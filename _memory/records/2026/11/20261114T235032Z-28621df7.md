Decision (G2): chord-core parses ChordPro with its own hand-written, line-oriented parser and no longer depends on chordsheetjs. The dependency is removed from package.json, the pnpm catalog and the lockfile; the docs/STACK.md row is `removed (G2)`, and scripts/g1-contract.test.ts asserts that no source imports chordsheetjs. Serialization is hand-written to a canonical form specified in docs/CHORDPRO.md.

Alternatives considered:
- (1) Whole-song ChordSheetJS 18 parsing, with quirks documented as normalizations.
- (2) A line classifier plus ChordSheetJS per lyric line, with `#` escaped as `\#`.

Rationale: ChordSheetJS whole-song parsing is lossy on save.
- A mid-line `#` starts a Comment and swallows the rest of the line, including chords.
- `label="…"` lives in Tag.attributes, not in the value.
- Grid, abc, ly and textblock sections produce unmapped Literal lines.
- An empty tab section yields a phantom line.
- Syntax errors throw at locations that point at the following line.

A line-oriented parser makes "one source line = one SongLine" true by construction. Diagnostics then point at exact lines, and no recovery loop is needed.

Consequences:
- The spec, not a library, defines behaviour. Normative rules live in docs/CHORDPRO.md and are guarded by the G2 contract test.
- Kit-owned text (ORCHESTRATION.md, layer strings) still says "parse/serialize via ChordSheetJS" until an agent-kit re-emit.
- Lookup tables keyed by tag names must use Object.hasOwn or a Set; see the pitfall record.

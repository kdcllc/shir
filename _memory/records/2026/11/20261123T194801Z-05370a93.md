Pitfall (g12-follow-ups): implementer and test-writer subagents ran regression/mutation proofs IN PLACE on real repository files, despite "scratch copy, never in place" in every context package.

What went wrong: in one task the agent deliberately mutated the real VisualEditor.tsx; in another a `cd <scratch>` failed silently inside a compound shell command, so the following `sed -i` mutated the real App.tsx. Both restored the file, but concurrent group members could have observed the mutated file, and the proof records were unverifiable.

How it was detected: the agents disclosed it in their reports; the orchestrator verified byte-identical restoration against the pre-group snapshot sha256, and the evaluators re-ran the proofs in their own scratch copies.

How to avoid: in proof instructions require `set -euo pipefail` and `cd <scratch> && …` (or absolute scratch paths for every edit), and have the orchestrator compare per-group sha256 snapshots after every spawn (the snapshot+hash fence method caught nothing stray here only because the files were restored). A related evaluator lesson from the same goal: a proof that mutates two sites at once can hide that one site is untested — one mutation site per proof.
